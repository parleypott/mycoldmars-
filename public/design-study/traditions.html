The Human Element · design study · breaking the house style

Four ways out.

One story — the Zapotec woman mapping the wind turbines swallowing her village — rendered four different ways, each in a named tradition, all built from your fonts and your palette. Same content, four aesthetics, so the only variable is the taste.

What we're leaving behind: cream paper, Georgia serif, one orange accent, tidy mono eyebrows, double rules. The literary broadsheet. It's become a reflex — and it's the exact look the whole design world now reads as generic. Every direction below is a deliberate break from it.

The Title Sequence what I'd pick

Saul Bass · Pablo Ferro · Criterion Collection · A24 · Rodrigo Corral
What it isImage-first, not text-first. The scouted frame fills the screen, graded to your teal; the title lands in vermilion Sailing Club like a film's opening card; a mono strip carries the facts. Letterboxed. The trench becomes a reel.
Why it fitsThese are films-in-waiting, and this is literally your existing brand — the “Island Called Beautiful” treatment — made into the whole tool. Real people, real places, cinematic. Nothing is more you.
The riskIt leans on the photograph. Stories without a strong frame need a fallback (a duotone plate, a map). We'd have to solve the “no image yet” state.

02

The Field Terminal

Bloomberg Terminal · Teenage Engineering · Dieter Rams / Braun · NASA mission control
What it isAn instrument, not a document. Near-black ground, Roboto Mono everywhere, the AI score as a giant Söhne Breit readout, the two blues as data categories, orange as the only alert. A scouting console.
Why it fitsIt's a tool with a hundred-plus items — the console register is honest, and it's maximally far from cozy paper. The dense spreadsheet already leans this way; this commits.
The riskIt can read cold for stories about human loss. The warmth has to come entirely from the photography and the writing, not the chrome.

The Overprint Press

Emory Douglas · Tibor Kalman / Colors · OSPAAAL posters · Grapus · Reza Abedini
What it isCheap-print, spot-colour, political. Cream as newsprint stock; vermilion and teal overprinted with deliberate misregistration and halftone dots; a woodblock headline; mono as wire copy. Rough, urgent, tactile.
Why it fitsThe trench is full of resistance and dispossession — Zapotec defenders, Sahrawi camps, Kanak independence. A press aesthetic gives those stories heat and dignity instead of a museum hush.
The riskLoud. Pushed too far it competes with the journalism and starts to feel “designed.” Discipline keeps it a press, not a poster.

The Wayfinding System

Massimo Vignelli / Unimark · Otl Aicher (Munich '72) · Lance Wyman (Mexico '68)
What it isSignage confidence. Off-white and near-black, the continents as a colour-coded rail (you already do this), a big Söhne Breit index number, a pin pictogram, mono labels. The world's stories as a transit system you navigate.
Why it fitsA hundred-plus stories across five continents IS a wayfinding problem, and the colour-coding is already in the tool. Systematic, calm, scalable.
The riskClosest to the “safe Swiss” zone — it can tip corporate. It needs the photography and one hot accent to keep a pulse.

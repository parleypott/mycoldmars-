Locked language

“The world is more beautiful and complicated than the news lets on.”

“It's not about getting people to agree. It's about getting them to care.”

Exit feeling: less alone — understanding (empowerment) plus connection (care).

The Spine - The viewer's exit feeling: LESS ALONE. Johnny's gloss: understanding (empowerment) plus connection (care) is what equals less alone. Both must be present. - The soul sentence: THE WORLD IS MORE BEAUTIFUL AND COMPLICATED THAN THE NEWS LETS ON. - The mission sentence: it's not about getting people to agree, it's about getting them to care. - The information-ecosystem critique (outrage, numbness) is background radiation now, not the pitch. - Johnny's own transformation stays behind the camera. He appears as the audience's body: guide, learner, companion. Never the subject.

The Aperture (what qualifies) - CORE ANCHOR: any human story that makes the abstract world concrete. Threat is ONE flavor, not the genre. A place in motion qualifies as much as a place in danger. - JOY IS HUNTED, not tolerated: towns reinventing themselves, craft renaissances, festivals that bind, absurd triumphs, ingenuity under constraint. - URBAN IS IN: delivery riders, apartment blocks, megacity neighborhoods, startup scenes. The human element is not rural by definition. - THE WEST IS IN: US and Europe qualify fully. The passport is not the premise. - PROTAGONIST UNIT: vary deliberately across the canon: single embodying person (a Jaba), a community, a relationship (split family, two sides of a line). - BIGGER ISSUE: usually present (geopolitics, climate, economics, technology), but a pure human story can carry alone sometimes. The bigger issue may also be universal (grief, aging, leaving home) rather than geopolitical. - TIMELINESS: not a news chaser. When news breaks somewhere, note it and hunt the story for AFTER the cameras leave. Evergreen anchors. - FEASIBILITY: ignored at hunt time. Ideas first; producers solve access later. (Flag obvious impossibilities in a line, never filter on them.) - RED LINE (absolute): no active war zones. Everything else is judgment, not rule. (Post-conflict, frozen conflict, and edges of conflict are allowed.)

The Palette - Season mix: NOT all loss/threat. Exact ratio unset; the correction is against elegy-as-default. Hunt weeks should carry visible emotional variety. - Calibration signal: of four test stories he chose "a dying glacier village's last winter" as most exciting. The elegy still rings deepest for him. So: loss stories stay first-class; the mandate is to SURROUND them with other registers, not replace them. - HUMOR IS ESSENTIAL. The show should be funny sometimes. Hunt stories with comedy in their bones, not just gravity. - NEVER PRODUCE: despair. Every loss story needs the thread that leads somewhere: resistance, adaptation, memory, absurdity, love. If a story is only downhill, it fails the palette.

The Form (affects hunting) - Explainer lives as short interludes, chapter breaks. So a story must WORK as lived experience first; the explainer layer is seasoning, and a story chosen only for its explainer potential is a miss. - Anthology episodes are open: three short human stories around one theme. The hunter may propose anthology BUNDLES (tag: bundle) when finds rhyme. - Form opportunities flagged occasionally, only when the story demands its form.

Hunter Mechanics - CADENCE: weekly, Mondays. 8-12 lighter finds per week; Johnny triages. - GEOGRAPHY: roam free. Surprise him. (His curiosity orbit: borders, islands, mountains, infrastructure, Asia — is gravity, not a fence.) - SOURCES: all lanes: local-language press, academic fieldwork and ethnographies, NGO/UN field reports, Reddit/forums/diaspora communities, local documentary and photo projects, Newpress community submissions. Multi-language search always. - PENDING: set up a public tip line where people submit stories for vetting. - VERDICTS: star rating (1-5) plus free notes. Notes feed DNA amendments gently. - RESURRECTION: nothing dies. Changed circumstances resurface old stories.

Dossier Requirements Every dossier carries: place + coordinates; the human anchor (who, specifically); the story in ~120 words written to be FELT; the bigger issue in one short paragraph; why it's a Human Element story (which DNA lines it serves); emotional register tag (loss / resilience / joy / absurd / mystery / connection); speculative YouTube title and a one-line thumbnail concept (packaging thinking from day one, always); sources with at least two independent links; scouted imagery when findable.

The Test (applied to every find, in order) 1. Is there a person or community whose daily life IS the story? 2. Does the abstract world become concrete through them? 3. Would a viewer finish feeling less alone (understanding + connection)? 4. Can it be retold at dinner in one sentence? 5. Does it avoid despair-without-thread? A find needs all five. Nothing else is disqualifying.

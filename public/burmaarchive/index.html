Gaps & proxies

Where no faithful depiction exists — and the closest usable stand-in.

  • Faces of the early kings (Anawrahta, Kyansittha, Bayinnaung, Alaungpaya, Bodawpaya) Use proxies and label them as proxies: later Konbaung court paintings and temple donor-portraits (Commons: Art of the Konbaung dynasty), the kings' own monuments (Bodawpaya → Mingun Pahtodawgyi; Anawrahta / Kyansittha → Shwezigon and Bagan donor figures, the Mahamuni), and modern commemorative statuary. For Bayinnaung, a 1568 plan of Bago and Thai temple murals of his campaigns.
  • The Bagan / Pagan era (9th-13th c.) court and daily life Lean on the architecture and the in-situ murals as the period's authentic visual culture (Commons per-temple categories; EFEO historic mural photographs). Frame them honestly as 'the world these kings built', not 'portraits of these kings'.
  • Pre-1850s Konbaung court life, from the Burmese side The closest eyewitness renderings are Burmese-made parabaik court scenes (British Library; SOAS; Bodleian watercolours) and the 29 plates in Yule's 'Mission to the Court of Ava' (1858).
  • The three Anglo-Burmese Wars as one conquest map (1826 / 1852 / 1885) Redraw: take a clean Konbaung-at-peak base (Commons, CC-BY-SA) and overlay three dated bands — 1826 Treaty of Yandabo (Arakan + Tenasserim), 1852 (Lower Burma / Pegu), 1885 (Upper Burma + Mandalay). Every boundary is well documented; this is a design job, not an archive hunt.
  • The moving royal capitals as one 'travelling capital' map Redraw one Irrawaddy-basin base with six dated pins. Note the visual beat: Ava/Inwa, Amarapura and Mandalay cluster within ~20 km of each other; Pegu/Bago is the southern (Mon / Toungoo-era) outlier.
  • The small kingdom vs the large British Burma shape Redraw: a Konbaung-extent polygon (Commons) under the British Burma administrative outline (MIMU-derived, or a Rumsey / LoC 1900 colonial map). The silhouette difference is the argument in one frame.
  • Watts & Skeen and D.A. Ahuja as named photographers Route through Wikimedia Commons ('D.A. Ahuja Burma', 'Watts and Skeen') plus the Myanmar Photo Archive for provenance. Treat dealer / blog scans as leads to verify, not citable primaries.

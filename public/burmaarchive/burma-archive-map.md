# Burma, in the archive
### Where to find imagery for every visual the script asks for — for the Newpress archive producer

_32 archive needs · 9 categories · 49 repositories · every link checked · compiled 17 August 2026_

## How to use this

Every RED-marked archive cue in the script (the `archive-req`, `map-need` and inline bracketed calls) has been pulled out and grouped into the categories below. Under each category: first *what the script asks for* — quoted, with its row number — then the real repositories that hold that imagery, with a direct link, how to search, and a rights note. Every URL was fetched and checked on 17 August 2026. A few institutions (British Museum, Library of Congress, Smithsonian, Cambridge, HathiTrust) block automated checks but load in a browser — flagged where they appear.

**The honest read.** For ancient Burma, availability splits hard. Buddhism, temples and pagodas, colonial-era photography, King Thibaw's fall, and the modern political era are richly documented. But the faces of the early kings do not exist: photography reached Burma only in the 1850s, and King Mindon (r.1853-78) is the earliest Burmese monarch ever photographed. For everyone before him there is no contemporary likeness, only stone and later paint. The Bagan-era record survives almost entirely as temple murals. Where a faithful depiction doesn't exist, the Gaps & Proxies section names the closest usable stand-in.

  - https://www.bl.uk/stories/blogs/posts/buddhist-rebirth-in-different-planes-of-existence

## Gaps & proxies

Where a faithful contemporary depiction likely does not exist — and the closest usable workaround.

### Faces of the early kings (Anawrahta, Kyansittha, Bayinnaung, Alaungpaya, Bodawpaya)

- **The gap:** No contemporary likeness of any kind exists. Photography only reached Burma in the 1850s-60s, and Burmese court convention never produced individualised royal life-portraits. King Mindon (r.1853-78) is the earliest Burmese monarch ever photographed — he is the break point.
- **The workaround:** Use proxies and label them as proxies: later Konbaung court paintings and temple donor-portraits (Commons: Art of the Konbaung dynasty), the kings' own monuments (Bodawpaya → Mingun Pahtodawgyi; Anawrahta / Kyansittha → Shwezigon and Bagan donor figures, the Mahamuni), and modern commemorative statuary. For Bayinnaung, a 1568 plan of Bago and Thai temple murals of his campaigns.

### The Bagan / Pagan era (9th-13th c.) court and daily life

- **The gap:** The only contemporary visual record of the era is the temple murals themselves — and those depict Buddhist and Jataka scenes, not named rulers or court life. No portable images survive.
- **The workaround:** Lean on the architecture and the in-situ murals as the period's authentic visual culture (Commons per-temple categories; EFEO historic mural photographs). Frame them honestly as 'the world these kings built', not 'portraits of these kings'.

### Pre-1850s Konbaung court life, from the Burmese side

- **The gap:** Photography arrived with the colonial apparatus, so there is no eyewitness photograph of the independent court.
- **The workaround:** The closest eyewitness renderings are Burmese-made parabaik court scenes (British Library; SOAS; Bodleian watercolours) and the 29 plates in Yule's 'Mission to the Court of Ava' (1858).

### The three Anglo-Burmese Wars as one conquest map (1826 / 1852 / 1885)

- **The gap:** No single period map shows all three annexations on one sheet — the British drew them war by war.
- **The workaround:** Redraw: take a clean Konbaung-at-peak base (Commons, CC-BY-SA) and overlay three dated bands — 1826 Treaty of Yandabo (Arakan + Tenasserim), 1852 (Lower Burma / Pegu), 1885 (Upper Burma + Mandalay). Every boundary is well documented; this is a design job, not an archive hunt.

### The moving royal capitals as one 'travelling capital' map

- **The gap:** No period map plots Bagan → Pinya → Ava/Inwa → Amarapura → Pegu/Bago → Mandalay together — they are centuries apart.
- **The workaround:** Redraw one Irrawaddy-basin base with six dated pins. Note the visual beat: Ava/Inwa, Amarapura and Mandalay cluster within ~20 km of each other; Pegu/Bago is the southern (Mon / Toungoo-era) outlier.

### The small kingdom vs the large British Burma shape

- **The gap:** No single period sheet overlays the two shapes — which is the whole point the script wants to make visually.
- **The workaround:** Redraw: a Konbaung-extent polygon (Commons) under the British Burma administrative outline (MIMU-derived, or a Rumsey / LoC 1900 colonial map). The silhouette difference is the argument in one frame.

### Watts & Skeen and D.A. Ahuja as named photographers

- **The gap:** There is no clean, single institutional repository for either under their own name; their output survives folded into Beato / Klier attributions and scattered across postcards.
- **The workaround:** Route through Wikimedia Commons ('D.A. Ahuja Burma', 'Watts and Skeen') plus the Myanmar Photo Archive for provenance. Treat dealer / blog scans as leads to verify, not citable primaries.

The Thesis Dig

Seven steps to find the one thing the show is actually about. Ninety minutes. Pen, not screen.
Before you start.
  • A thesis is not a slogan. It is the controlling idea: the thing every episode secretly obeys, whether you named it or not.
  • You already have one. Five episodes made it, unconsciously. This is excavation, not invention. Dig, don't dream.
  • Do not edit while you generate. Ugly handwriting, crossed-out words, arrows in the margin. That is the work looking healthy.
  • Alone first. Then, if you want, hand the blank to two people who know the work and compare digs. Do not average them.
  • The hard part is not finding a true sentence. It is killing the four other true sentences so one can stand.
STEP 01

The evidence15 min · look back before you look forward

The thesis is already in the footage you love most. Not the prettiest shot. The moment that felt like the reason you flew there.

For each episode, write the one moment that made you feel you were doing the real thing. One line. Instinct, not analysis.

STEP 02

The viewer of one10 min · forget the millions

You cannot make something for everyone. You can make it for one real person and let it travel. Aim narrow; it carries wide.

Name one real person you know you want this show to reach. Not a demographic. A face.

Their name

They just finished an episode, alone, late. Not what they learned. What they feel, or say out loud, or text a friend. Write the first answer. Then ask "and underneath that?" and write again. Then a third time. The third answer is usually the true one.

STEP 03

The enemy10 min · fast, don't think

Every show worth making is against something. Not a topic. A force. What you refuse is often sharper than what you want.

The force this show exists to fight is

If we never made it, the world would have a little more

The kind of storytelling I would never make, even for the numbers, is

Name a real show or style in that last blank. The boundary you draw is the edge of your thesis.

STEP 04

The four altitudes15 min · break it, find the weak floor

Most theses are strong at two altitudes and generic at the other two. Find the generic floor and rebuild it until only your show could stand on it.

Write your current one-liner for the show, however rough:

Now split it into four layers. Fill what yours says at each:

The world — what territory?
The method — how do you enter it?
The feeling — what happens to the viewer?
The why — what is it ultimately for?

The test: which layer is weakest, the one any show could say? Cross it out. Rewrite it three times below until it could only belong to you.

STEP 05

The only test10 min · un-copyable or bust

If a competitor could put their logo on your thesis and it would still be true for them, it is not yours yet.

The Human Element is the only show that

Can you name another show that also does exactly that? If yes, cross it out and go again. Keep going until the blank is true and no one else can claim it.

STEP 06

Converge · three candidates15 min · genuinely different, not rephrased

Do not write one sentence three ways. Write three different souls for the same show and make them fight.

From everything above, write three thesis sentences. One line each. No semicolons. A stranger could repeat it. Give each a name.

STEP 07

The kill10 min · your body knows before your head

Run all three through four tests. Mark each A / B / C where it passes. The one that survives, or fails most gracefully, is the thesis.
The dinner test. Could a viewer retell it in one sentence the next morning, without the footage?
The episode test. Does it govern an episode you have NOT made yet? Name it.
The exclusion test. Does it tell you what NOT to make? Name a good story it rightly rejects.
The goosebump test. Read each aloud. Which one moves your chest? Do not overrule the body.

Validate in the wildthe following week · do not trust the room

A thesis that cannot shoot a scene is not a thesis yet. Three field tests before you believe it.

1. The eyes. Say your one sentence to three people who do not know the show. Watch their faces, not their words. Note who leaned in.

2. The picker. Hold it against your next three Story Hunter dossiers. Does it cleanly pick winners and reject the near-misses? A real thesis sorts.

3. The scene. Try to write the cold open of the Nepal episode using your thesis as the only instruction. If it writes the scene, it is real. If it just sounds nice, dig again.

Keep this page. When the show drifts, it is almost always because an episode stopped obeying the sentence you signed. Come back, read it aloud, and either the episode is wrong or the sentence has grown. Both are worth knowing.

The Talent Brief Showrunners, senior producers, and brains worth picking for The Human Element

This search optimized for the two gaps in Season 1: episode architecture (thesis threading, act structure, character arcs that complete, tension that holds instead of dissolving) and senior field producing (access, characters, scenes engineered before the crew lands). It hunted the reference craft worlds directly, the Bourdain machine, the Trafficked machine, the Whiteley arc factory, the Gelb cinematic school, Nutopia's big canvas, and Frontline's access rigor, then verified every person against primary sources. Every credit below survived fact-checking; where the original claim was wrong, this brief carries the corrected version. The list is split into three tiers by what to do with them: showrunner candidates who could run the season, senior producers who could own episodes and field blocks, and brain-picks whose knowledge is worth a plane ticket even if they are not hireable. Availability signals are current as of September 2026.

The Showrunner Candidates

Morgan Fallon

Fallon is an LA-based executive producer, director, and cinematographer who spent a decade as the field craft of the Bourdain machine: he shot, directed, and produced across the 103-episode run of Parts Unknown (2013-2018) after starting with Bourdain on No Reservations in 2008, roughly 60 shows in 50 countries. He has two Emmy wins from seven Primetime nominations, the 2013 cinematography award for the "Myanmar" episode and the 2018 Outstanding Informational Series win as a producer of Parts Unknown. He then proved he could run a host-driven social series as director and EP of United Shades of America with W. Kamau Bell, directed and EP'd Hunting History with Steven Rinella (January 2025), EP'd Sneaker Wars for Hulu, and most recently showran the five-part CNN Original Craig Ferguson: American on Purpose, which finished airing in July 2026. He takes commercial work through the Search and Destroy Media director roster; he is not a principal there.

He is the strongest single answer to both halves of the gap. A decade of engineering scenes and character access at travel-doc pace, plus recent proof at the showrunner level, and he knows exactly how to build an episode around a host's presence in a place, which is the structural problem The Human Element has to solve every episode. He apprenticed under Michael Mann, which is where the craft standards come from, and he has called the "West Virginia" episode his proudest work because the crew went in without condescension and let people be fully human. That is nearly a paraphrase of this show's thesis, and it is the opening line of the outreach note. His three-hour Tim Ferriss interview (#597) is the homework before the call. Timing is unusually good: he likely just wrapped the Ferguson series and works project to project.

Approach: Hire conversation, top of the list. Approach directly or through the Ferriss orbit; Search and Destroy handles his commercial representation only.

Darren Foster

Foster co-created and executive produced Trafficked with Mariana van Zeller for National Geographic, the nine-time Emmy-winning series that ran from December 2020 through its final season in 2026, and he is a founding partner of Muck Media, the LA and Lisbon shingle with a Nat Geo first-look deal signed in 2023. Before that he co-directed Science Fair (Sundance Festival Favorite 2018, SXSW Audience Award, Emmy winner) and directed American Pain for CNN Films and HBO Max (Tribeca 2022), the culmination of more than a decade of his own opioid-epidemic reporting. Trafficked wrapping puts him at an inflection point: van Zeller spun her new venture The Hidden Third out of the Muck team, and Foster has said publicly that the Trafficked infrastructure will keep producing stories.

He is the closest living craft analog to what this show wants to be. He engineered a repeatable format that sends one journalist into dangerous global worlds and returns with character-driven, access-first episodes that hold tension for a full hour, at prestige level, across nine Emmy-winning seasons. That is episode architecture and senior field producing fused in one person, in exactly the lane the brief names. The distinguishing fact for outreach: he is married to van Zeller, Muck is literally a family shop, and American Pain capped over a decade of long-arc investigative reporting, which is the version of patience this show is betting on.

Approach: Hire conversation or co-production. Approach via Muck Media; frame The Human Element as the next Trafficked-caliber field-doc franchise. The Nat Geo first-look could turn this into a co-pro conversation, which is worth knowing before the call.

Sandy Zweig

Zweig was the executive producer and showrunner of Parts Unknown across its 2013-2018 run, the person who held the machine together at Zero Point Zero while directors were in the field, with EP Emmy wins in 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2018, and 2019, the last two covering the posthumous seasons she shepherded after Bourdain's death. She was also EP of No Reservations, EP and showrunner of Season 2 of My Next Guest Needs No Introduction with David Letterman, and EP/showrunner of United Shades of America (Emmy nominations 2021 and 2023). Most recently she was co-executive producer on Season 2 of Solo Traveling with Tracee Ellis Ross for Roku, which premiered July 2026. She is freelance, active, and has no announced next project.

If the core failure mode is tension that dissolves, she is the most direct answer available, because holding thesis, act structure, and season architecture across a flagship travel-doc series was literally her job for six years, twice. And the market timing favors Newpress: she is currently taking co-EP work on smaller shows, so a flagship offer is a step up, not a step down. The distinguishing fact for outreach: she accepted the 2018 Creative Arts Emmys after Bourdain died and said Tony was the public face of the show but knew there were lots of people making it happen. She was the chief of those people.

Approach: Hire conversation, showrunner. She operates as an independent showrunner-for-hire, which is exactly the employment shape a Season 1 startup needs. Approach directly.

John Henion

Henion is Boardwalk Pictures' showrunner and director on Welcome to Wrexham (2022-present), the industry's best current example of thesis-threaded episode architecture in a docuseries; he personally shares the show's 2023 Emmy win. He came up as a producer on Season 1 of Chef's Table (2015), showran Fearless for Netflix (2016), and EP'd Dear... for Apple TV+ and The Playbook for Netflix (both 2020). Between Wrexham seasons he directed and EP'd The Tides That Bind: Inside Alabama Football for Fox Nation (2025) and EP'd the Apple TV+ Messi docuseries. The honest constraint: Wrexham's Season 5 aired mid-2026 and FX renewed the show through at least Season 8, so he is locked in long term.

The reason he is on this list anyway is that his stated method is the show's missing skill in its purest form. He went on record arguing that finding one connective theme is the most valuable tool for making an unscripted story cohesive, discover the emotional center and build the episode around it, and Wrexham episodes are constructed exactly that way. He speaks both the Gelb cinematic language and the serialized character-arc language, which almost nobody else does. The distinguishing fact for outreach is that podcast interview on theme as the central creative throughline; it maps almost word for word onto this show's people-first thesis.

Approach: Probe timing and exclusivity for a hire, but expect a no on full time. At minimum, a premium brain-pick on theme-driven episode architecture; he demonstrably takes outside gigs between Wrexham seasons.

Ruth Shurman

Shurman was Nutopia's showrunner on Limitless with Chris Hemsworth (Nat Geo/Disney+, 2022), running the six-part premium series from pre-production to delivery over roughly three years, and she just came off Pole to Pole with Will Smith (premiered January 2026), where she was EP on location including the Antarctica leg. Before the big-canvas era she spent almost twenty years at the BBC as a director and series producer: Surgeons: At the Edge of Life, The Secret History of My Family, and My Dad, the Peace Deal and Me, which won her a Grierson and a BAFTA nomination. Celebrity Hunted won her a second Grierson. She is a freelance EP/showrunner represented by Natalie Spanier at Stern & Wild, London-based, and also takes consultant-EP work in development and post.

She is the rare person with both halves of the brief in one CV: the Nutopia work is exactly the big-canvas cinematic reference the show names, celebrity-fronted and multi-strand at premium scale, while the BBC years are access-led human journalism, the intimacy engine. She started as a journalist before moving into film, which means her arc is Johnny's lane run in reverse. She would also bring a non-US, female perspective to the season room. The distinguishing fact for outreach: reference the just-aired Pole to Pole Antarctica shoot and her journalism-first origins, and the note reads as genuinely researched.

Approach: Hire conversation, showrunner, via her agent at Stern & Wild. The most straightforwardly hireable name on this tier; the practical question is a London-to-US remote arrangement.

The Senior Producers

Helen M. Cho

Cho is a two-time Emmy-nominated director/producer in New York who was a producer on all twelve seasons of Parts Unknown (2013-2018), including field-producing the "West Virginia" episode with Morgan Fallon directing. She then ran a show of her own as EP and showrunner of Take Out with Lisa Ling (HBO Max, 2022), which IndieWire named one of the best docuseries of that year. She produced and directed on United Shades of America, produced on the Letterman Netflix show and later directed its Caitlin Clark episode, directed the South Korea episode of Kara Swisher Wants to Live Forever for CNN, and was co-EP on Hunting History with Steven Rinella (2025). She works freelance, project to project, and is an active mentor with Unlock Her Potential.

She is the classic field-producer-to-showrunner arc: she engineered Bourdain scenes on the ground for twelve seasons, then proved the architecture half by running a culturally specific host-driven series end to end. That covers the senior field producing gap with showrunner range on top, and her recent pivot into directing adds a weapon. She and Fallon are a known working pair, which opens the option of approaching them as a founding unit rather than two separate hires. The distinguishing fact for outreach: she leads with being the daughter of a nail salon worker and a USPS letter carrier, the working-class Korean American story that powered Take Out, and it is exactly the intimate-experience-reveals-the-system instinct this show runs on.

Approach: Hire conversation, senior field producer or co-EP. Approach directly, and consider packaging with Fallon.

Marcela Gaviria

Gaviria has made more than fifty hours of documentary for PBS Frontline since 1994, when her first credit was the Pablo Escobar film Godfather of Cocaine. The run includes Drug Wars (2000), In Search of Al Qaeda (2002), Obama's War (2009), the three-part America and the Taliban (2023), the Peabody-winning Separated: Children at the Border, and The Gang Crackdown, one of the films behind Frontline's 2019 duPont-Columbia Gold Baton. Her full trophy shelf runs to seven Emmys, four Peabodys, and a Polk. She works out of Rain Media in New York, Martin Smith's shop, and her latest film, The Crown Prince and the President, premiered on PBS in June 2026, so she is likely between projects right now.

She is the gold standard for the exact skill the hardest episodes are missing: securing access in hostile, complicated places, Afghanistan, Colombia, the border, and structuring that access into acts with characters whose arcs complete. She brings journalistic depth the prestige-TV names cannot, and there is no extraction problem because Frontline producers have always worked film to film as independents. The distinguishing fact for outreach: she is a native of Colombia whose first Frontline job was telling her home country's hardest story to an American audience, then she spent five years freelancing from Colombia. Place-based, human-first journalism is not a pitch to her, it is her biography.

Approach: Hire conversation, senior or supervising field producer on a per-episode basis, aimed at the Myanmar and Mauritania-class episodes. Approach directly.

Arielle Kilker

Kilker is a two-time Emmy winner who sits at the exact intersection of the two reference worlds: she cut Chef's Table in its early seasons, was supervising editor of Last Chance U (2016-2020), and then was developer, producer, co-director, and supervising editor of Cheer (2020-2022), winning an ACE Eddie for it, with both her Emmy wins tied to that show. She was co-EP of Golden for Peacock, producer and supervising editor of Navajo Police: Class 57 for HBO, and co-EP of Netflix's Marines with Amblin and Lucky8, which premiered November 2025. She is currently directing her own verite documentary feature, her first as solo director.

In the Whiteley system, the arcs that complete are constructed by the supervising-editor-producer layer, and she is that layer, credited in development and direction, not just in the cutting room. If the show's core failure mode is tension that dissolves, she is the specialist in why arcs hold, on paper, in the field, and in the timeline, and she can institutionalize that discipline inside Newpress rather than just perform it. The distinguishing fact for outreach: she deliberately framed Cheer as a character study rather than a sports story and assembled a largely female editorial crew to do it; that character-first framing conviction is the hire.

Approach: Hire conversation, supervising producer or co-EP. She moves project to project as a freelancer; the feature in production means the conversation is about timing and structure, possibly part time across the season's edit.

Adam Leibowitz

Leibowitz has been inside Greg Whiteley's One Potato unit continuously since Mitt (2014), the field half of the arc machine: producer then supervising producer on Last Chance U, co-director and co-EP of Last Chance U: Basketball, supervising producer on Cheer (sharing its 2020 Emmy win), producer on Wrestlers, and now executive producer on America's Sweethearts: Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders, which Netflix renewed for a fourth season in August 2026, meaning he is presumably in production right now.

He is on this list because he embodies the field posture Johnny wants the show to have. Whiteley's crews embed with subjects for months, engineering access, trust, and scenes far in advance, and the internal description of the job, nature photographers for people, is almost exactly the presence-first, being-with discipline in the show's thesis. He walks into closed institutions and comes out with characters whose arcs complete. The honest read: he has never left the One Potato orbit in fourteen years, so a full extraction is unlikely. The distinguishing fact for outreach: his whole career came from one leap-of-faith unpaid internship that led to meeting Whiteley, and his film obsession started when his father took him to a restored print of Vertigo as a kid.

Approach: Brain-pick first, with a long-shot hire or consulting conversation timed between America's Sweethearts seasons. His EP elevation makes outside consulting more plausible than it used to be.

Jeff Allen

Allen is the only candidate who was senior on both reference shows. He produced on Parts Unknown at Zero Point Zero, sharing the 2017 Primetime Emmy nomination for Outstanding Informational Series, then went to Trafficked with Mariana van Zeller, where he rose from producer-director to executive producer, personally directing and shooting episodes including the South Africa highway-robbery hour that was Emmy-nominated for Outstanding Hard News Report. He also produced Elvis Goes There for Epix. He has filmed in more than 55 countries. With Trafficked ended, he showran Off Day for Uninterrupted and produced The Hidden Third podcast, and his own site states he is currently available for consulting and producing engagements.

He is the literal bridge between the two craft worlds in the brief: host-driven intimacy and scene engineering learned inside the Bourdain machine, then applied to dangerous-access geopolitical field production on the closest existing show to this one's risk profile. Field-producing a Myanmar or Mauritania episode is squarely his lane, and he is not a desk EP, he shoots and directs in the field himself, which maps onto Johnny's own shooter-director style. The distinguishing fact for outreach: the Emmy-nominated South Africa hour he directed and shot himself, plus the explicit availability note on his own site.

Approach: Hire conversation, senior field producer or field EP, per episode. He is independent and explicitly available; approach directly. Probably the fastest yes on this list.

Aldo Kane

Kane is an ex-Royal Marines commando sniper, one of the youngest in the forces when he joined at 16, who founded Vertical Planet, the company productions contract for safety, security, and logistics in remote and hostile environments. His screen record runs across exactly the tier this show references: on-screen expedition leader on One Strange Rock, where he abseiled into Nyiragongo's erupting lava lake on camera, expedition and safety lead on Expedition with Steve Backshall, presenter of the BBC investigation Tigers: Hunting the Traffickers, safety and logistics lead on Arctic Ascent with Alex Honnold and James Cameron's OceanXplorers, and co-lead of the Apple TV+ series The Wild Ones (premiered July 2025). Fellow of the Royal Geographical Society, thirteen world-first expeditions.

He answers the physical-possibility half of senior field producing: getting a crew and characters safely into extreme, access-hard places and making the impossible shot filmable there. Karnali roads, Sahara crossings, Myanmar borderlands, this is the de-risking layer none of the edit-room names bring, and Vertical Planet is explicitly built to be contracted into other people's productions, so there is nothing to extract. The distinguishing fact for outreach: he is the guy who made an erupting volcano safely filmable, and his whole career is making directors' impossible shots possible.

Approach: Hire per shoot, field EP and safety/logistics lead, contracted through Vertical Planet. UK-based, deploys globally. Book him for the hardest one or two shoots of Season 1 rather than the season.

The Brain-Picks

Tom Vitale

Vitale spent sixteen years with Bourdain (2002-2018), over a hundred episodes across A Cook's Tour, No Reservations, The Layover, and Parts Unknown, starting as a tape logger and working up through post-producer to director, collecting five Emmys along the way. He directed more than seventy field trips including the notoriously chaotic Congo episode Bourdain called a career favorite. After Bourdain's death he largely stopped making television and instead wrote In the Weeds (Hachette, 2021), a memoir that documents the production method itself, fixers, chaos, engineered scenes, intimacy in volatile places. No new directing project has surfaced since, which makes him both maximally available and maximally candid.

Nobody alive has articulated the Bourdain episode-construction method more completely: how a thesis, a place, engineered scenes, and a host's interiority get braided into an act structure. The book is essentially this show's production bible written by someone else, and a structured conversation with its author can pressure-test every Season 1 episode architecture before a single flight is booked. There is also real hire upside if the conversation reveals he wants back in. The distinguishing fact for outreach: open with the Congo shoot or the book's tension between the TV machine and the real moment, and the note lands as informed rather than generic.

Approach: Brain-pick first, and early, this is the cheapest, highest-yield conversation on the list. Gauge appetite for a directing or field-EP role before pitching anything full time.

Brian McGinn

McGinn is the format architect behind the two structures closest to this show's brief. He developed Chef's Table for television with David Gelb, EP'd every season, and directed nine episodes, including on Chef's Table: Legends (April 2025), where he personally selected the four legend chefs; the franchise has three Primetime Emmy nominations and five James Beard Awards and is renewed for more seasons. He co-created Street Food with Gelb, directed Amanda Knox (two Emmy nominations), directed Trial by Media, and is a founding partner of Supper Club with Gelb and Jason Sterman, whose slate includes Stan Lee and Olivia Rodrigo: driving home 2 u. He remains on PRETTYBIRD's commercial roster.

He is not realistically hireable, he runs his own company, but he is the single highest-value brain on format architecture. Chef's Table is single-subject cinematic biography with operatic arc completion; Street Food is place plus political economy told through intimate vendors, which is structurally the sibling of the Palau, Nile, and Mauritania episodes. How Street Food threads a city's politics through one person's story is precisely the problem this show has to solve ten times a season. Supper Club's independence makes a paid format-design consult or light EP attachment plausible in a way a big-company showrunner could never accept. The distinguishing fact for outreach: he gives craft interviews readily and just spent the Legends press cycle reflecting on ten years of elevating nonfiction to cinematic prestige.

Approach: Brain-pick or consulting EP, possibly a project hire on a pilot block. Approach through Supper Club.

Mariana van Zeller

Van Zeller was the correspondent and EP of Trafficked, which ran on National Geographic from December 2020 to its final season in September 2025 and is billed by Nat Geo itself as a nine-time Emmy winner; she won a Peabody in 2010 for The OxyContin Express. In 2026 she made the same jump Johnny is making: she founded The Hidden Third, an independent company spanning journalism, video, podcasting, books, and live programming, built to keep the Trafficked team together, with the podcast launched October 2025 and Season 2 now out. She signed with Mosaic in January 2026 for representation across everything. Portuguese-born, five languages, runs the company between LA and Lisbon.

She is a double brain-pick. First, on craft: how Trafficked engineered access and held episode tension inside dangerous worlds. Second, on structure: how to build an independent institution around a single on-camera journalist, which is the Newpress question stated exactly. She is not a hire, she is on-camera talent building her own shingle, but she is a potential strategic ally and co-production partner for the show's most dangerous-access episodes, and right now, mid-launch, is when she is most reachable and most candid. The distinguishing fact for outreach: the company name comes from the economists' term for the third of the global economy running on black and gray markets, and her stated ambition for the new venture, think cinematically with strong storytelling and careful sound design, is nearly the pitch for this show.

Approach: Brain-pick and possible alliance, creator to creator from Johnny directly. Not a job offer.

Garrett Kennell

Kennell is the co-creator, director, and Chief Creative Officer of Challenge Accepted, Michelle Khare's YouTube series, and the clearest living proof that prestige episodic craft can run natively on YouTube at exactly Johnny's scale. Under him the show became the first YouTube creator series to petition its way onto the Primetime Emmy ballot (2025), won the 2026 Telly Gold for non-scripted series plus earlier Streamys, and now produces feature-length episodes that premiere at film festivals. He directed Khare's early breakout films and the MK Ultra series, is a Cannes-screened short filmmaker, and is married to Khare. He is fully embedded in her operation and not available, but he is actively doing press about the craft and the model.

The reason to get him on the phone is operational, not editorial: the entire full-time Challenge Accepted team is seven people, Kennell directing, one head of production, three editors, one EA, and that seven-person shop produces TV-prestige nonfiction with months of pre-production per episode. That is the solo-creator-to-elite-studio scaling problem Newpress is solving, already solved once, inside another creator's shop. The distinguishing fact for outreach is exactly that: how do you get TV craft out of seven people. He has answered versions of it in interviews and would answer it better peer to peer.

Approach: Brain-pick and advisory only, do not attempt to poach. Warm creator-to-creator outreach from Johnny, framed as two craft leaders comparing pipelines for premium episodic work in the YouTube-native economy.

Sequencing

Call Vitale first. He is the cheapest conversation, the most available, and one afternoon with him sharpens every later interview because he can articulate the method everyone else practices. Then run the two hot showrunner windows in parallel: Fallon, who likely just wrapped the Ferguson series, and Zweig, who has no announced next project. Both windows close. Foster third, since his conversation may become a co-pro negotiation and benefits from a sharper ask. On the producer side, open with Allen, who is explicitly available, and Cho, who packages naturally with Fallon. Gaviria, Kilker, Kane, and the remaining brain-picks slot in behind the season calendar.
